Sakura Notes

Privacy policy

Effective 25 November 2026

In short. Without an account, your work stays in your browser and Sakura receives none of it. With an account, your documents and settings are stored so they follow you across devices, and no one else can see them unless you send someone a copy. No ads, no analytics, no tracking, nothing sold. If something goes wrong, a short error report (never your documents) helps fix it; you can switch that off.

Sakura Notes ("Sakura", www.sakura-notes.com) is run by Robin Singh Rajawat, an individual based in Denmark, who is the data controller for the personal data described here. Contact: privacy@sakura-notes.com.

Using Sakura without an account

Everything you create stays in your browser's own storage on your device. Sakura's servers receive none of it.

Like any website, the server that delivers the page (GitHub Pages, run by GitHub) receives the technical information every browser sends: your IP address, browser type and the page requested. GitHub uses it to deliver the site and protect it from abuse. Sakura doesn't receive these logs. Legal basis: legitimate interest in running the website (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)).

When you open the diagram editor, it loads from diagrams.net (run by JGraph Ltd), which sees the same technical information. The diagram itself is edited in your browser.

If you use AI or send feedback without an account, or something goes wrong in the app (an error report), the sections on those below apply.

Sending someone a copy

With Send a copy, you type the exact email address of another Sakura user. If an account with that verified email exists, they get their own copy of the document, together with your name and email so they know who sent it, and a notification; nothing is added to their documents until they choose to add it. If no account uses that email, you're told so. This means anyone who knows your exact email can find out that it has a Sakura account; nobody can browse or search for other users. Once added, a copy is theirs: deleting your account doesn't remove copies you've already sent. Copies sent to you are deleted with your account.

Until 19 November 2026 Sakura also had public profiles (name, email and profile picture that others could search for). They were removed; your old profile is deleted the next time you sign in, and any left from accounts that don't sign in again are deleted by 19 December 2026.

AI features

Sakura Hosted AI: the text you ask AI to work on goes through Sakura's server (a Cloudflare Worker) to an AI provider Sakura chooses, currently Groq or Google (Gemini). Sakura doesn't store that text. It keeps only a count of your requests for the day, which expires within 48 hours. The provider processes the text under its own terms.

Your own AI key: the text goes straight from your browser to the provider you chose, with your key, under that provider's terms. Sakura's server isn't involved. Your key stays on your device.

Legal basis: providing the feature you asked for (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)). Don't send AI anything you wouldn't share with that provider.

Connecting an AI assistant

If you're signed in, you can let an AI assistant that supports MCP, such as Claude or ChatGPT, read your documents. Nothing is connected unless you do it: you add Sakura's address as a connector in the assistant, then allow it on a Sakura page. The assistant can read (list your documents and folders, read a document with its notes, tables and threads, search them, see what is still open and what changed, and read your To-Dos). Documents in Trash are left out. If you also tick "send new drafts to your inbox" when you connect it, it can put a new document in your Sakura inbox when you ask it to; nothing is added to your documents until you preview it and tap Add, and it never changes, adds to or deletes your existing documents.

Each time the assistant asks, Sakura's server (a Cloudflare Worker) reads what it asked for from your account and passes it to the assistant. Sakura doesn't store or log that content. A draft it sends is kept in your inbox until you add or decline it, like a copy someone sends you. To read as you, the server keeps your Firebase sign-in for that connection, encrypted so that only the assistant's own access token can unlock it, along with the assistant's name and when you connected it. What the assistant reads goes to its provider (for example Anthropic or OpenAI) and is handled under that provider's terms, not Sakura's.

You can see every connected assistant and disconnect it at any time in Settings → Account → AI assistants; disconnecting deletes its stored sign-in and ends its access at once. A connection also ends after 30 days without use, after 180 days at most, and when you delete your account. Legal basis: providing the feature you asked for (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)).

Downloading a PDF file

When you're signed in, Share → Export as → PDF sends a print-ready copy of the document to Sakura's server (a Cloudflare Worker), which turns it into a PDF using Cloudflare Browser Rendering and sends the file back. Nothing is stored apart from a daily count of PDFs, which expires within 48 hours. Without an account, the PDF is made by your own browser and nothing leaves your device.

Feedback and error reports

If you send feedback, Sakura stores your message, the email address you choose to give (optional), your account ID if you're signed in, your browser type and the page address. Feedback is kept until it's been dealt with and is then deleted.

Error reports are on by default and can be switched off in Settings → About → Error reports. When something goes wrong in the app, it sends a report to Sentry (run by Functional Software, Inc., stored in Sentry's EU data region): the error message (shortened), where in Sakura's code it happened, the app version, your browser and operating system, and the page address without anything after ? or #. It never includes your documents, your account, your email, a record of what you clicked or typed, or your IP address (Sentry is set not to store it). Reports are kept for up to 90 days. Before 20 November 2026 these reports were off unless you turned them on; if you used Sakura before then, the app told you once before sending any.

Legal basis: legitimate interest in finding and fixing problems that stop Sakura working (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)). You can object at any time by switching error reports off.

Cookies and storage on your device

Sakura sets no cookies, with one exception: while you connect an AI assistant, Sakura's server sets a short-lived cookie that ties the request to your browser, so no other site can approve it for you. It expires within ten minutes. Apart from that, Sakura keeps the following in your browser's own storage on your device, only so the app works:

  • your documents, settings and a local backup copy (local storage and IndexedDB);
  • if you sign in, your sign-in session, kept by Firebase Authentication (IndexedDB);
  • a copy of the app's own files, so it opens quickly and works offline (the browser's cache, through a service worker);
  • a few flags that last only while the tab is open, such as whether you closed the welcome screen (session storage).

All of it is strictly necessary for features you use, so Sakura doesn't ask for consent, and none of it is used to track you. It stays on your device until you clear this site's data in your browser. If you're signed in, let your work finish syncing first; anything not yet synced would be lost. Signing in with Google happens on Google's own pages, under Google's privacy policy.

What Sakura doesn't do

  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking pixels, no profiling.
  • Your data is never sold or used for anyone else's purposes.
  • No cookies for tracking. Sakura uses your browser's storage only for what it needs to work: your work, your settings, and keeping you signed in (plus the short-lived cookie while you connect an AI assistant). That's why there's no cookie banner.

Who processes data for Sakura

  • Google (Firebase Authentication, Cloud Firestore; Gemini for Hosted AI)
  • Cloudflare (the server for Hosted AI, PDF files and connected AI assistants)
  • Sentry (error reports, EU data region)
  • Groq (Hosted AI)
  • GitHub (hosting the website)
  • JGraph / diagrams.net (the diagram editor, when you open it)

Some of these companies are based in the United States and may process data there. Where they do, the transfer relies on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework or the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can:

  • See and take your data: Settings → Data & backup → Download all data saves everything in one file.
  • Correct it: edit it in the app, or ask.
  • Delete it: delete documents at any time, or Settings → Account → Delete… to erase everything your account has in Sakura's cloud.
  • Ask for processing to be restricted, object to processing based on legitimate interest, and object to error reports by switching them off.

For anything else, email privacy@sakura-notes.com; you'll get an answer within one month. You can also complain to the Danish Data Protection Agency (Datatilsynet, datatilsynet.dk) or the authority in your own country.

Security

All connections are encrypted (HTTPS). Database rules let each account read only its own data, and these rules are tested automatically. Content is cleaned of anything that could run as code when it's loaded. No system is perfectly secure; if a breach affects your data, you'll be told as the GDPR requires.

Children

Sakura isn't intended for children under 13, and an account shouldn't be created for them.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes too. Significant changes are announced in the app before they take effect.
